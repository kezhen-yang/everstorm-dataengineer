## 1. DDP versus DDU — the single most important distinction

Two words decide whether you get a bill at your door.

**DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** means we have already paid the import duty and
VAT. The price you paid at checkout is the total price. Nothing further is
owed.

**DDU (Delivered Duty Unpaid)** means the carrier pays the duty on your behalf
at the border and then collects it from you, usually with a brokerage or
"advancement" fee on top, before they will release the parcel.

| Destination | Basis | What you pay on delivery |
|---|---|---|
| EU / EEA | **DDP** | Nothing |
| United Kingdom | DDU | Import VAT + DPD brokerage fee |
| Canada | DDU | GST/HST + provincial tax + carrier brokerage |
| Australia / New Zealand | DDU | GST if over the de minimis threshold |
| Japan / Singapore | DDU | Consumption tax + duty if over threshold |
| United States (domestic) | n/a | Nothing beyond sales tax charged at checkout |

The EU moved to DDP on 18 May 2025 with Revision 4.0 of the shipping policy.
Before that date EU orders shipped DDU like everywhere else. Orders placed
before that date are governed by the terms in force at the time of purchase.

**This is the most common source of duty complaints we receive.** A customer in
the UK or Canada reads the EU paragraph, concludes shipping is duties-paid, and
is then surprised by a carrier invoice. The EU line does not extend to the UK.
The UK left the EU customs union and is treated as a separate destination for
every purpose in this document.
